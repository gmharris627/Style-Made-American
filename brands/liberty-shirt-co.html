Worth it?

Yes — and not as a close call. Liberty Shirt Co. makes custom dress shirts in the Bronx, at prices that actually make domestic, made-to-measure apparel accessible, run by people whose entire family history is this craft. The founding story isn't a brand narrative; it's just the biography of who built the thing.

Commitment

Every shirt is cut and sewn in the Bronx after you order it — there is no pre-made stock, no offshore overflow waiting in a warehouse to fill gaps. The made-to-order model is the whole operation: if you don't order it, it doesn't get made. That's a genuinely higher bar than brands that manufacture domestically for flagship items while quietly sourcing the rest abroad. The founders' deep manufacturing background, combined with their hands-on Bronx workshop, makes the "Made in USA" claim credible rather than aspirational.

Character

Marc Fromowitz spent years as COO of High Bar Shirt Co., a custom shirt operation serving TV broadcasters and pro athletes — a company that collapsed during Covid. Rather than pivot into something else, he and his wife Stacey launched Liberty Shirt Co. on July 4, 2020, a date they chose on purpose. The result is a small workshop making OCBD and dress shirts alongside patriotic graphic tees: no lifestyle campaigns, no photography teams, just people who know how to make shirts making shirts. That clarity of purpose is itself distinctive in a category crowded with brands that treat domestic manufacturing as an aesthetic.

Ethics

No meaningful labor or ethical concerns have surfaced in independent coverage of Liberty Shirt Co. The made-to-order model is structurally cleaner than most apparel operations — there's no opaque supply chain to obscure, no overproduction pressure pushing corners to cut. The reasonable open question is whether wages and working conditions in the Bronx workshop meet living-wage benchmarks, something the brand hasn't publicly documented in detail. We've reached out to Liberty Shirt Co. for more information on their labor practices and will update this section when we hear back.

Heritage

Marc and Stacey are each grandchildren of tailors and garment workers — a lineage that traces back to 1898, when Marc's great-grandfather emigrated and opened a tailoring shop on 173rd Street in the Bronx. By the early 2000s, offshore competition had gutted domestic shirt manufacturing; by 2006, the family factory was gone entirely. Liberty Shirt Co. is a conscious attempt to reclaim that thread. The borough, the craft, and the family backstory aren't branding choices — they're the actual context the company grew out of, and the brand has stayed consistent with that identity since its founding.

Value

Bundle pricing runs roughly $110–$130 per shirt for a set of three (around $333 total); individual shirts land closer to $259 depending on fabric. For a domestically made, custom-fitted dress shirt, that's genuinely competitive — comparable made-to-measure options from brands like Proper Cloth or Indochino touch $150–$200 and up without domestic labor anywhere in the equation. Liberty Shirt Co. sits at the intersection of custom fit and American manufacture without requiring a corporate expense account. The graphic tees, at around $24, function as an accessible entry point and come in well below the going rate for US-made casualwear.
